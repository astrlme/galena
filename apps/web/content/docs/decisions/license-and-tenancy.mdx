---
title: Apache-2.0, single tenant
description: Why Galena is permissively licensed and deploys one workspace per account.
---

## Problem

Galena's main use is running it in your own AWS account, and many companies don't allow AGPL code
inside. Hosting many tenants adds isolation, billing and noisy-neighbour work that doesn't help
someone running their own copy.

## Decision

License under the Apache License 2.0. Deploy one workspace per installation, into the operator's
own AWS account. Every table still carries `workspace_id`.

## Alternatives

| Option | Why not |
| --- | --- |
| MIT | Just as permissive, but with no explicit patent grant and nothing about trademarks |
| AGPL-3.0 | Many companies avoid it, which works against running Galena in your own account |
| A source-available license | Not open source, and a barrier to the same adopters |
| A multi-tenant service | Scope, cost and security surface |

## Consequences

- Anyone may use, change and redistribute Galena, in open or closed products, keeping the license
  and its notices.
- Contributors grant a patent license for what they contribute.
- The license grants no rights to the Galena name.
- No hosted offering to maintain. Adding tenancy later means scoping sign-in and filtering rows,
  not rewriting the schema. See [Global identity tables](/docs/decisions/identity-tables/).

## Revisit when

There is demand for a hosted version.
