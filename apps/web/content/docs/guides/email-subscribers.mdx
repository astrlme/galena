---
title: Email subscribers
description: Let visitors subscribe to incident and maintenance emails.
---

Email subscriptions need SES. Set `email` in your stage config and finish the DNS and production
access steps in [Self-hosting](/docs/getting-started/self-hosting/#point-dns-at-it).

<Callout>
A deployment without `email` sends no email, so its status page shows no **Subscribe** and the API
refuses subscriptions. In development the workers write emails to `apps/workers/.local/mail/`
instead of sending them, and subscribing works.
</Callout>

## What a visitor sees

1. **Subscribe** on the status page opens a dialog (a bottom sheet on phones) asking for an email
   address. With more than one component, "Only some components" lets them pick.
2. They get an email with a confirmation link. The link opens a page on your status page with a
   **Confirm** button; nothing is confirmed until they press it, so mail scanners that open links
   can't subscribe anyone.
3. From then on they get an email for every incident and maintenance event that touches what they
   follow. Each email has a plain-text part, a link to the incident, and a one-click unsubscribe
   link. Mail clients that support one-click unsubscribe show their own button too.

Without JavaScript, the form is a plain section at the foot of the page and still works.

## Limits that protect inboxes

- One confirmation email per address every 10 minutes, however often the form is sent.
- At most 200 confirmation emails an hour across the page.
- Unconfirmed addresses expire after 7 days.
- An address that hard-bounces or complains is suppressed for good: SES stops sending to it, and
  subscribing again does nothing.

## Managing subscribers

**Subscribers → Email** lists every address, masked (`a***@example.com`), with its state, what it
follows and when it subscribed. Admins can delete an address; it stops getting email at once.
The dashboard never shows a full address, and there is no export.
