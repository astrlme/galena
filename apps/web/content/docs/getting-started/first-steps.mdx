---
title: First steps
description: From an empty workspace to a status page that tells people when something breaks.
---

<Steps>
<Step>

### Add components

**Components** are what your users care about: "API", "Dashboard", "Webhooks". Add them in
**Components**, group related ones (a group shows as a heading on the page), and move them up or
down into the order your page should show. See [Components and the page](/docs/concepts/components/).

</Step>
<Step>

### Add monitors

In **Monitors**, add an HTTP monitor for each public endpoint and attach it to a component. Three
regions check it every minute, and when two agree it is down, its component's status changes and
the page republishes within seconds. See [Add a monitor](/docs/guides/monitors/).

</Step>
<Step>

### Connect notifications

In **Subscribers**, add a Slack incoming webhook for your team's channel, or an outgoing webhook
for your own systems. Visitors subscribe to email themselves from the page's **Subscribe**
button. See [Email subscribers](/docs/guides/email-subscribers/) and [Slack](/docs/guides/slack/).

</Step>
<Step>

### Post your first incident

When something goes wrong, open **Incidents**, choose **Publish incident**, pick the affected
components and their status, and write the first update. Subscribers hear about it, and the page
shows it at the top. See [Run an incident](/docs/guides/incidents/).

</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout title="Two-factor">
Sign-in asks for a TOTP code on any account that has two-factor on, but the screen to turn it on
arrives with the Settings section. See the [roadmap](/docs/roadmap/).
</Callout>
