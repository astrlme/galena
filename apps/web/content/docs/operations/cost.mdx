---
title: Cost
description: What a deployment pays for at rest, what grows with use, and the choices that keep it small.
---

Galena is designed to cost a few dollars a month when nothing is happening. Your bill depends on
your regions, your number of monitors and how often people look at the page, so set an AWS budget
with alerts and check it after the first month.

## Fixed monthly charges

| What | Why it exists | List price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One KMS key | Created by the foundation stack for envelope encryption; nothing uses it yet | $1 a month |
| Three Secrets Manager secrets | The database credentials, and the origin secret plus its replica in the page region | $0.40 each a month |
| Aurora storage | The database's data, billed per GB-month | small for a status page |
| CloudWatch Logs | One log group per Lambda, kept 30 days | grows with traffic |

## What stays in free tiers at small sizes

| Service | Use at rest |
| --- | --- |
| Lambda | Three probe runs a minute, the evaluator per result, and the API per request |
| DynamoDB | Provisioned at no more than 25 read and 25 write units per account and region, the always-free allowance |
| SQS | One message per check, sent in batches |
| CloudFront and S3 | The page, its data files and the dashboard |

The free-tier sizes are AWS's; check them for your account. A deployment with many monitors, or a
status page under heavy traffic, outgrows them.

## Aurora: billed while awake

Aurora Serverless v2 is billed per capacity-unit hour while it runs, and pauses at zero capacity
after five idle minutes. At rest it wakes for the hourly uptime rollup and whenever someone uses
the dashboard. Nothing in Galena polls it more often than hourly; that rule is what keeps the
database from being the largest line on the bill.

## trigger.dev

trigger.dev bills by runs and compute on its own plans. The hot path never calls it per check,
only on a state change. At rest a deployment runs three tasks an hour (the rollup, the publish it
triggers, and the HTML rebuild), plus one dispatch and a publish per change, and one run per
notification sent.

## Email

SES charges per email sent, $0.10 per 1,000 at list price.

## Choices that keep it small

- **No NAT gateway.** Aurora is reached through the Data API, so nothing needs a route out of the
  VPC.
- **Nothing polls Aurora.** The cold path is event-driven; the only schedule that reads the
  database runs hourly.
- **Checks never touch the database or trigger.dev.** They go from SQS to DynamoDB, inside its
  free allowance.
- **A static page.** Visitors cost CloudFront and S3 requests, not compute.
